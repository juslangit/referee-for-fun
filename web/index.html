One official · one reputation

Six sports, six ladders to climb

Each sport asks you to judge something none of the others do, and each one climbs from a community hall to a building modelled on a real Malaysian venue. Pick one, then flip between the bottom of its ladder and the top.

Badminton

Two line judges, diagonal corners

Top of the ladder: an arena drawn from the Axiata Arena: two tiers of blue seats, glass suites and a screen cube over the floor.

A shuttlecock with its own drag model, and two officials who are wrong about as often as a coin on the close ones. Agree with a mistake and the blame is shared in plain sight.

What you are actually deciding

Five things the chair does to you

In · out · let · four faults

Every call has a recorded truth

IN, OUT, LET, and the four faults: net touch, carry, double hit, obstruction. Every fault has both a recorded truth and something visible on court: the net shakes, the ball sticks on the racket for a moment, a player genuinely reaches over.

Which means ignoring one is itself a lie.

Wrong about as often as a coin

Two line judges, one chair

They sit at diagonally opposite corners, so the pair see all four boundary lines between them. Agree with a mistake and the blame is shared with an official in plain sight. Overrule them and the hall has watched two officials disagree, with only your call left standing.

National level and above

Hawk-Eye makes it public

Two reviews a side per game, a successful one handed back. Players sometimes challenge a call you got right. Otherwise a challenge would be a verdict announced in advance.

It is the one moment in the game where the truth goes up on the screen.

One number, three seconds

A reputation out of a hundred

Your name, carried between matches and shared across all six sports. It appears only when it moves, for three seconds, and then it is gone. It never tells you whether a call was believed, only what the night has cost you so far.

What gets an umpire caught is not being wrong. It is being wrong in the same direction every time.

Four of them. None is money.

A reason to lie, never a bribe

An envelope makes the umpire a criminal, and then the only interesting question, would you?, has been answered for you. Instead: a tournament that needs a name in the final, a panel marking whether anything happened, a player you refereed badly last season, and your own first wrong call pulling you to even it up.

The reward is always for the result, never for the lie.

Getting past the security prompt

Referee For Fun is made by one person, not a company with an Apple Developer certificate and a Microsoft signing key, so both systems say they cannot check who made it. You only have to tell them once. The source is on GitHub if you would rather read it first.

On macOS 15 Sequoia and later, right-click → Open no longer gets past Gatekeeper; the way through is System Settings. For older versions see below.

  1. Open the .dmg, drag Referee For Fun to Applications and open it. macOS stops it. Click Done.

No Open Anyway button? macOS 11 to 14? The Terminal?

The Open Anyway button only stays in Privacy & Security for about an hour after macOS blocks the app. If it is not there, open the app once more, click Done, and look again.

On macOS Big Sur to Sonoma, right-click the app in Applications and choose Open, then Open again in the dialog. That is all.

From the Terminal, on any version, this removes the download flag so the first launch is not stopped at all: xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/Referee For Fun.app"

There is no installer: the .exe is the whole game. Put it in a folder of its own. It writes its save next to your user profile, not beside itself.
