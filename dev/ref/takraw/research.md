## A. Court and equipment

**Court**
- **Size:** 13.4 m × 6.1 m, clear of obstacles up to 8 m above the floor. That 8 m is the minimum ceiling height (Law 1.1).
- **Lines:** at most 0.04 m wide, drawn *inwards* from the edge of the court measurement. The lines are inside the court area, so a ball on the line is in (Law 1.1).
  - A served ball is valid if it lands "inside the boundary of the two net tapes and boundary lines" (Law 10.13).
  - STAC's rewrite of the rules says a ball whose outer edge is over the line is in.
- **Centre line:** 0.02 m wide, dividing the court in half (Law 1.2).
- **Quarter circles:** one at each end of the centre line, with the corner where sideline meets centre line as the centre. Radius 0.9 m, drawn from the sideline to the centre line (Law 1.3).
- **Service circles:** one per half. Radius 0.3 m, line 0.04 m wide. The centre is 2.45 m from the back line and 3.05 m from each sideline, so it sits in the middle of the width (Law 1.4).
- **Which edge of a circle line the radius is measured to:** the 2024 rulebook does not say.
  - The 2011 rulebook put the line *outside* the radius ("drawn outwards from the edge of the 0.3 m / 0.9 m radius").
  - STAC's 2023 rewrite puts it *inside* ("inward from the outer edge").
- **Free zone:** 3.0 m clear beyond the sidelines and baselines, up to the low A-shaped advertising boards (Law 1.5 and glossary).
- **Doubles and quad courts:** same 13.4 × 6.1 m court, but with no service circles and no quarter circles. Quad lines are 0.05 m wide, including the centre line.

**Posts and net**
- **Posts:** 1.55 m high for men and 1.45 m for women; radius no more than 0.04 m (0.05 m in quad). They stand 0.3 m outside each sideline, in line with the centre line (Laws 2.1 and 2.2).
- **Net material:** fine nylon cord, mesh 0.06 to 0.08 m (Law 3.1).
- **Net size:** 0.7 m deep and at least 6.1 m long (Law 3.2).
- **Tapes:** 0.05 m tape, doubled, along the top and bottom, with a fine cable through the top tape pulled tight at the tops of the posts (Law 3.4).
- **Side bands:** two 0.05 m bands fixed vertically above each sideline. They count as part of the net (Law 3.3).
- **No antennae:** there are none in the rules; the side bands mark the crossing space.
- **Net height:**
  - Men: 1.52 m at the centre, 1.55 m at the posts.
  - Women: 1.42 m at the centre, 1.45 m at the posts (Law 3.5).
  - STAC gives the posts as 1.55 m for both men and women, which contradicts ISTAF.

**Ball (Law 4)**
- **Material:** synthetic fibre, one woven layer, 12 holes and 20 intersections. It may have a synthetic rubber or soft covering if ISTAF approves it.
- **Circumference:** 0.41 to 0.43 m for men, 0.42 to 0.44 m for women.
  - My own conversion to diameter: about 13.1 to 13.7 cm for men and 13.4 to 14.0 cm for women.
- **Weight:** 170 to 180 g for men, 150 to 160 g for women.
- **Colour:** single-coloured, multi-coloured or luminous are all allowed.
- **History:** woven synthetic balls replaced rattan in Thailand in 1982 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepak_takraw).
- **On TV:** a **yellow Marathon** synthetic ball. I saw it in Bernama's photos from Titiwangsa, 2025. Marathon MT.201 is marketed as ISTAF/ASTAF approved (https://sportztrack.com/ph-spztrk/marathon-mt201-official-tournament-sepak-takraw-ball/).

## B. Formats and positions

**Regu (3 players)** (Laws 5, 7, 9)
- **Names:** the **Tekong** (server) at the back, the **Left Inside** and the **Right Inside**. Together the two front players are the "Inside" players.
  - Common English names are feeder/setter for the left and killer/striker for the right (Wikipedia).
  - Malay names are *apit kiri* (left) and *apit kanan* (right) (https://sinarbestari.sinarharian.com.my/interaktif/tiga-posisi-penting-sepak-takraw).
  - Malaysian press labels players "feeder / tekong / killer", for example Farhan Adam, Syahir Rosdi and Azlan Alias (https://www.freemalaysiatoday.com/category/highlight/2025/05/15/malaysia-beat-thailand-to-clinch-asian-cup-sepak-takraw-regu-title).
- **Squad:** 3 on court plus up to 2 reserves.
  - 2 substitutions per set, made by showing a Substitution Card while the ball is dead.
  - A player who was substituted out may come back in the same set.
  - Team event: 3 regus, 9 to 12 players.
- **Positions at service:**
  - **Tekong:** non-kicking foot inside the service circle, in ready position, before the referee calls the score. The kicking foot can be anywhere (Law 9.2).
  - **Serving side's two Inside players:** each inside their own quarter circle (Law 9.3).
  - One Inside player throws the ball to the tekong.
  - Neither Inside player may lift a foot, step on the quarter circle or centre line, or cross or touch the net while the ball is being thrown (Law 11.1.2).
  - **Receiving side:** free to stand anywhere in its own court (Law 9.4).
  - **Everyone:** free to move as soon as the tekong kicks the ball (Law 10.12).
  - Neither side may shout or gesture to distract after the score is called (Law 11.2.1).

**Doubles (2 players)** (Double Laws 5, 9, 10.4)
- The server stands behind the baseline with both feet, **throws the ball to himself**, and must not touch the baseline or enter the court, even on the follow-through.
- The non-serving player stands still inside the court.
- No player may raise their arms above shoulder height until the serve is made.
- The two players **take turns** as server each time their side serves.
- Squad: 2 players plus 1 reserve.

**Quad (4 players)** (Quad Laws 5 and 9)
- Any of the four may serve, from behind the baseline with a self-throw. The other three stand still inside the court, with the same rule against raising arms.
- Team event: 2 regus of 4, 8 to 12 players.

## C. Scoring

**Current rules (2024, the same for regu, doubles and quad)** (Laws 10 and 12)
- Every fault gives a point to the other side (rally scoring).
- A set is won at **15**. At **14-14** the referee announces "Setting up to seventeen (17) points" and the **first side to reach 17 wins**. There is no two-point margin: 17-16 is a valid score, and 16-14 does not end the set.
  - STAC's rewrite confirms "no longer need to win by a minimum of 2 points".
  - Real results show 17-15 (FMT, 18 May 2025).
- **Best of 3 sets**, with a **2-minute break** between sets.
- There is no separate tie-break format. Teams change ends before every set, and **in the third set when one side reaches 8** (Law 10.8).
- **Coin toss:** the Court Referee tosses in front of both captains. The winner chooses to serve first or picks an end; the loser takes the other choice (Law 8).
- **Service:** the side chosen at the toss serves first, then **service alternates after every single point, whoever wins it**. This continues at 14-14 (Laws 10.3 and 10.5).
  - The side that received first in one set serves first in the next (Law 10.6).
  - In doubles, the two partners also alternate as server (Double Law 10.4).
  - The Quad rulebook contradicts itself: Law 10.3 says every point and Law 10.4 says every three points. The Bernama and STAC sources both say every point.
- **Time-outs:** one per side per set, up to 1 minute, called only by the team manager or coach while the ball is dead. Only two non-players may come to the baseline (Law 13).
- **Injury time:** up to 5 minutes in total per side (Law 14).
- **15-second rule:** after "POINT, (team name)", the tekong has 15 seconds to be ready. The first delay gets a warning; a repeat is a fault (Laws 10.9 and 11.1.3).

**Evidence that service alternates every point:** the Astro Arena score bug from the 2026 World Cup shows the serve arrow switching between points (for example THA had it at 2-5 and MAS at 1-3). That pattern only fits one serve each.

**Older rules**
- **ISTAF 2013:** sets to 21. At 20-20, win by two or first to 25. Best of 3. Each side served **3 times in a row**, alternating every 3 points and every point after 20-20. Ends changed at 11 in the third set.
- **ISTAF January 2011:** sets to 15, win by two or first to 17. **Best of 5** for regu and doubles (best of 3 for team event). 3 serves each. Ends changed at 8 in the deciding set.
- **1965 SEAP Games (Kuala Lumpur):** 15 points, best of 3, points scored only by the serving side ("service over").
- **Late 2023:** Malay Mail (5 November 2023) reported that ISTAF would *keep* 3 serves per rotation under the new 15-point system. The final 2024 rulebook and Bernama both say one serve each.

## D. Faults and who calls them

**Tekong's serving foot: the written rule is under review**
- **Written rule (2024):** it is a fault if the non-kicking foot is **lifted off the floor before the kicking foot touches the ball**, or **steps completely out of the service circle** (Law 11.1.4).
  - Older 2011 wording: "jumps off the ground… or the standing foot steps on the service circle line".
- **Proposed change:**
  - In September 2024 ISTAF proposed turning the circle into a foot marker only, so the tekong could lift or "fly" the non-kicking foot (https://www.bernama.com/en/news.php?id=2336751).
  - On 22 July 2025 the Secretary-General said the proposal would be finalised at a council meeting in Hat Yai, Thailand (https://berita.rtm.gov.my/arena/senarai-berita-arena/senarai-artikel/istaf-muktamad-cadangan-mansuh-peraturan-batal-servis-terbang/).
  - The Malaysian federation (PSM) opposed it (https://www.scoop.my/sports/265811/a-death-blow-to-sepaktakraw-heritage-psm-blasts-istafs-tekong-rule-overhaul/).
  - One low-reliability Malaysian site says it was used at the December 2025 SEA Games (https://ebantuan.my/sepak-takraw-sukan-sea/).
  - ISTAF's site still publishes only the 2024 rulebook.
  - **I could not confirm from ISTAF that the change was adopted.** It may be worth making this an option in the game.

**Serving side during service** (Law 11.1)
- An Inside player who is throwing plays with the ball (tossing it up, bumping it, handing it to the other Inside player) after the score is called.
- An Inside player lifts a foot, steps on the quarter circle or centre line, or crosses or touches the net during the throw.
- The ball is thrown before the referee calls the score:
  - the first time, it is re-thrown and the side gets a warning;
  - the second or later time in the match, it is a fault (Laws 10.11 and 11.1.10).
- The tekong breaks the foot rule above, or does not kick the thrown ball.
- The serve touches a player on the serving side, fails to cross the net, or crosses and lands out.
  - A serve that **touches the net but crosses and lands in is valid** (Law 10.13).
  - It must pass between the two side bands.
- A player uses a hand or arm for support while kicking, even without touching the ball, for example by pressing on the floor (Law 11.1.9).

**Both sides during play** (Law 11.3)
- Touching the ball on the opponent's side.
- Any part of the body crossing into the opponent's court, over or under the net, except as the follow-through of a kick or block.
  - If the body crosses *before* contact with the ball, it is illegal (glossary 21).
- **More than 3 touches in a row.** The rules set no limit per player, so one player may take all three touches (https://oursportinglife.co.uk/sepak-takraw/).
  - A ball that rolls unintentionally from one legal body part to another counts as one touch (Law 10.4).
- **The ball touches a player's arm.** The 2024 rulebook does not define "arm". Secondary sources say the arm runs from the shoulder to the fingertips, and ActiveSG says "arms or hands".
- Stopping or holding the ball under the arm, between the legs or against the body.
- The body or clothing (shoes, jersey, headband) touches the **net, a post or the referee's chair**, or lands on the opponent's side.
- The ball touches the **roof, a wall or any object other than the net**.
- A player uses an external object for support while kicking.

**Who calls it, and signals**
- The rulebooks do not divide calls between officials. The best signal source I found is the Thai Department of Physical Education's *Sepak Takraw Referee Manual* (2012, written with the Takraw Association of Thailand): https://pubhtml5.com/uaai/xobx/basic/
- **Assistant Referee** covers the net and the referee's blind spots. Signals:
  - **Centre-line crossing:** one hand points at the centre line, the other points to the side awarded the serve.
  - **Net touch:** one hand points at the net, the other points to the side awarded the serve.
  - **Ball passing outside the side band ("around" the net):** both hands meet, crossed, above the head.
  - **Ball touching an arm:** one hand raised, the other touches that arm, then points to the side awarded the serve.
  - **Ball out:** both arms spread out to the sides of the body.
  - **Ball in:** one hand points down at where it landed, the other points to the side awarded the serve.
  - **More than 3 touches:** a hand held above the head showing 4 fingers.
- **Line Referees** signal **only when the referee asks**:
  - **In:** one hand points down into the court.
  - **Out:** both arms bent, palms turned inward, held parallel to the body.
- **Referee** (on the chair) makes the final call by voice. The manual's call list is: STOP, GAME POINT / MATCH POINT, FAULT, LET, OUT, IN, NET, SERVE, POINT, WIN, CHANGE SIDE, TIMEOUT, FIRST SET / SECOND SET / TIE BREAK SET, SETTING UP TO 17 POINTS, plus "LINE REFEREE" and "ASSISTANT REFEREE" when asking them for a call.
  - The 2024 rulebook's own wordings are "POINT, (team name)", "Setting up to seventeen (17) points" and "REPLAY" for a let (Laws 10.9, 12.3 and 14.5).
- **Whistle:**
  - The 2024 Regu and Double rulebooks never mention one.
  - STAC says "There are no whistles in Sepak Takraw".
  - The Thai manual has no whistle.
  - Only the **Quad** rulebook uses one: the throw comes "after one long blow of the whistle" (Quad Law 10.12).
  - For regu, the referee's voice controls the game.
- **Video challenge:**
  - ISTAF's 2025 scoresheet has boxes for 3 challenges per side per set (CH1 to CH3). Each is recorded by type (SERVICE, NET or LINE) and result ("point reverse" or "point not reverse") (https://sepaktakraw.one/istaf-document-resources).
  - ISTAF said centre-line faults are not covered by the challenge system (https://www.nationthailand.com/news/sport/40066631).
  - Malaysia's team manager, however, said video was reviewed for exactly such a call in the 2026 World Cup final (https://thesun.my/sports/malaysia-wins-sepak-takraw-world-cup-after-thailand-walks-out/).
  - ASTAF tightened the challenge system for the Asian Games starting 20 September 2026 (https://sudutpandang.id/astaf-tightens-challenge-review-system-ahead-of-sepaktakraw-at-2026-asian-games/).

## E. Officials and positions

**2024 rulebook, Law 10.1**
- **Referee:** stationed at one end of the net.
- **Assistant Referee:** opposite the Referee.
- **Court Referee:** behind the Referee.
- **Line Referee 1:** to the right of the Referee, along the sideline.
- **Line Referee 2:** to the right of the Assistant Referee, along the sideline.
- **Official Referee:** supports the Referee from off court (Law 10.2). Only the Official Referee may suspend a match for disturbances, after consulting the Tournament Committee (Law 14.3).
- **Coin toss:** done by the Court Referee (Law 8).
- **Scoresheet:** the ISTAF 2025 scoresheet is signed by the **Referee and the Official Referee**.

**Not in the rules:** which post the referee sits at, and how tall the chair is.
- STAC: the referee sits on a "tall chair a couple feet behind the pole… eye level being about 2 feet above net". The Assistant Referee **stands** beside the opposite post.
- STAC: the two line judges stand at opposite corners, "at least 10 feet" out from the court.
- STAC: 2 to 4 scorekeepers flip score cards at alternate corners, and a ball fetcher sits behind the Assistant Referee.
- Thai manual: line referees sit, but only after the referee has sat on the chair. The Court Referee keeps the scoresheet.

**From 2026 World Cup broadcast frames**
- The referee's stand is a black metal platform with steps, next to the red padded post. The referee is well above the net.
- Behind it are white-skirted officials' tables with the ISTAF crest, staffed by officials in dark suits.
- Referees wear a **black blazer, white shirt, red tie and an ISTAF badge** on the breast pocket.

## F. Match procedure for cutscenes

**Before the match**
- **2024 rulebook:** Court Referee coin toss only. The 2013 rulebook also had a warm-up clause: the toss winner warms up first for **2 minutes**, then the other side; only 3 players plus 2 others may be on court with the official ball.
- **STAC's standard match protocol:**
  1. Teams enter and line up behind their baselines.
  2. The announcer introduces the players by number and name, then coach and manager.
  3. Teams walk **anticlockwise around the court**, shake hands over the middle of the net, and huddle.
  4. Court Referee calls "Captains" for the toss.
  5. Each side warms up for 2 minutes.
  6. The officials walk on in single file and are introduced.
  7. The referee welcomes the crowd, names the event and introduces **the team on his right first**, then the one on his left.
  8. The referee checks the officials and players are ready and gives the ball to the serving side.
  9. Opening call: "Canada serving… LOVE–ALL, FIRST SERVE". The serving side's score is always said first.
- **Thai manual:**
  - Officials stand to the right of the referee's chair while the teams and officials are announced, in this order: referee, assistant referee, 2 line referees, court referee.
  - The referee announces "players of both teams shake hands".

**During the match**
- Teams change ends before each set, and at 8 in set 3.
- 2-minute break between sets; 1-minute time-outs.
- STAC: the referee says "Change sides", players walk round the court anticlockwise, and the referee gives the set score, for example "First set, USA 21, Canada 18, two minutes rest".
- STAC: between rallies the ball is **rolled gently under the net**. Kicking or throwing it over is considered disrespectful.
- Only the captain may approach the referee (Law 15.2).

**After the match**
- The referee announces every set score and the winner, then says "shake hands please".
- Players walk round the court, shake the referees' hands at the posts, shake hands over the net, then acknowledge the crowd (STAC).
- Thai manual: line referees stand in front of the referee or assistant referee to receive the players' salute. The assistant referee collects the balls and takes them to the officials' table.
- The scoresheet is completed and signed by the Referee and Official Referee.

**Cutscene detail from 2026 broadcasts:** players walk out through a tunnel past a blue World Cup backdrop. Thai players do a *wai* (palms together) to the crowd.

## H. Player movement for animation

**Touches and kicks** (Malay names, with sources)
- **Sepak sila:** inside of the foot. Used to receive and to set ("mengumpan").
- **Sepak kuda / servis kuda:** struck with the top of the foot ("kekura"), foot pointed downward. This is the power serve, brought to international play by Thailand in 1995.
  - https://suria.my/terkini/sukan/ramai-masih-keliru-ketahui-3-terma-istimewa-termasuk-maniam-dalam-sepak-takraw/
  - ActiveSG describes the "horse kick" differently, as a high kick sending the ball backwards over the shoulder or head.
- **Sepak cungkil:** toe poke, for rescuing hard balls.
- **Sepak badek / simpuh:** outside of the foot.
- **Menapak / bola tapak:** sole of the foot, to push an attack or block.
- **Memaha:** thigh. **Mendada:** chest. **Membahu:** shoulder.
- **Tandukan / menanduk:** header with the forehead or the side of the head. There is also a header spike.
- Source for most of these: https://penahitamputih.wordpress.com/3-teknik-teknik-sepak-takraw/

**Serve sequence:** after the score is called, an Inside player tosses the ball underarm ("melambung") to the tekong, who kicks it (http://tmkstakraw.blogspot.com/2012/04/kemahiran-asas-dalam-sepak-takraw.html).

**Spikes** ("rejam" or "libas")
- **Roll spike (rejam gulung):** take off from one leg, turn the body over in the air toward the target, and kick the ball over the opposite shoulder with the other foot.
- **Sunback / scissor spike ("lipat maniam", "rejam gunting"):** take off from one leg, scissor the legs, and strike the ball over the shoulder or head. Used mainly by killers.
- **Half-roll spike.**
- **Side spike (rejam sisi).**
- Sources: https://www.activesgcircle.gov.sg/learn/sepak-takraw/techniques-and-skills-in-sepak-takraw and the Suria FM article above.

**Block (menghadang)**
- Jump with your **back to the net** and block with the legs or body.
- School coaching guides say to jump until the back is level with the net, or at least 0.5 m above the net. Both are secondary Malaysian school sources:
  - https://sepaktakrawrotan.blogspot.com/2020/06/kemahiran-asas-sepak-takraw.html
  - http://tmkstakraw.blogspot.com/2012/04/kemahiran-asas-dalam-sepak-takraw.html

**Typical rally:** receive (sepak sila, chest, thigh or head) → the feeder sets high near the net with sepak sila → the killer spikes (roll or sunback) → the opponents' Inside players jump to block. Up to 3 touches per side, and one player may take more than one of them.

**Jump heights:** I found **no primary measured figures**.
- Sunback landings create a higher peak ground-reaction force than half-roll landings, in 8 Thai national women players (https://he02.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/spsc_journal/article/view/262622).
- In a study of Japanese collegiate players, every hamstring strain was in the dominant leg and caused by the sunback spike, 31.3% of all injuries (PubMed 27626501).
- Estimated oxygen use during matches was 69.1% of VO2max for killers, 68.5% for tekongs and 56.4% for feeders (Jawis et al., *Br J Sports Med* 2005, PMID 16244191).
